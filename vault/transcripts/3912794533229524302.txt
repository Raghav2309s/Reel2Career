If you're a smart, ambitious person who doesn't know what you want to do with your life, here are five articles I'd read to start figuring out what's next. Reading these helped me quit investment banking and build a portfolio career across startups and content where I'm much, much happier. Number one, how to do what you love by Paul Graham, the founder of YC. What he says in this article about prestige is what made me realize I chose my career in banking for the wrong reason. He says, prestige is the opinion of the rest of the world. It is like a powerful magnet that warps even your own beliefs about what you enjoy. It causes you to work not on what you like, but what you'd like to like. Number two, Derek Cipher's blog post, hell yeah or no. This one's super short and simple. If you're not saying hell yeah to an opportunity, say no. Sometimes we have an easier time knowing what we don't want than what we do want. Number three, how to pick a career by Tim Urban. This one's fun. He has a concept called the yearning octopus. All these different parts of you that want different things at the same time. Your ego wants prestige, lifestyle wants comfort. The social tentacle wants the approval of your family and those around you. Tim says you have to figure out which yearnings are most important to you and untangle which are yours versus someone else's. Number four, make your work your calling by Arthur Brooks. This is a super practical one, especially if you're stuck in a job you don't love but can't leave yet. He says, don't wait for your calling to find you. Turn whatever work you're doing into your calling by focusing on what's intrinsically interesting and serving people around you. And the last one is Mark Andreessen's guide to career planning. He's the co-founder of A16Z and this one's my favorite, so I'll just read you some quotes. Do not plan your career. Instead of planning your career, focus on developing skills and pursuing opportunities. You should look at your career as a portfolio of jobs, roles and opportunities.
