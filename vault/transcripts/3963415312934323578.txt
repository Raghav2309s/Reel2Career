This might be a hot take, but I don't think big tech interviews are that hard. They're just formulaic, and once you know the formula, it's game over. For context, I pass multiple big tech interviews, and the questions are way less random than they seem. Trust me, you don't need to be a genius, you just need to know what to study. So here's part two of four years of CS knowledge, condensed so you can actually take action. First learn the patterns. Start with blind 75, then expand to neat code 150 or grind 75, focus on recognizing patterns rather than memorizing answers. Next use company specific resources. Check lead codes company tag questions, and github interview prep repositories instead of guessing what to study. Third search interview threads. Look up the company on Glassdoor, Reddit, and Blind to learn its interview format and commonly tested topics. Next do mock interviews. Practice solving problems out loud because knowing the answer and communicating it are two different skills. Finally, prepare your behavioral stories, match your skill in the drop description to an experience that proves you have it.
